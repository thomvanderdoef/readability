Failure to Disrupt: Why Technology Alone Can't Transform Education

Justin Reich · tsl.mit.edu ↗
F

Cliff Notes
  • Three genres of learning at scale — instructor-guided (MOOCs), algorithm-guided (adaptive tutors), and peer-guided — each with a long history and a recurring failure pattern.
  • The "curse of the familiar": new technologies get domesticated into existing classroom routines rather than transforming them. Schools absorb tools; tools don't reshape schools.
  • The Matthew effect: free learning technologies disproportionately benefit already-advantaged learners, often widening rather than closing gaps.
  • The trap of routine assessment: computers can grade what's easily right/wrong, which quietly biases tech-mediated learning toward routine tasks — the very skills automation devalues.
  • Core stance: incremental "tinkering" by educators, not disruption by outsiders, is how meaningful improvement actually happens.
